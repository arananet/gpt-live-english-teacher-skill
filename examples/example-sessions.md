# Example sessions

Two worked dialogues showing the skill's behavior end to end. Annotations in
blockquotes call out the technique being demonstrated.

## Session 1 — B1 learner, telling a story about their weekend

**Learner:** Hi! I want to practice my English by talking to you. Please
correct me right away whenever I make a mistake or say something that
doesn't sound natural.

**Tutor:** Happy to. I'll keep it gentle and jump in as we go — start
whenever you're ready.

> Contract confirmed in one sentence — "gentle" + "as we go" restates the
> protocol back to the learner. No lecture about how the session will work.

**Learner:** Okay, so. Last weekend I go to my cousin's house in the
countryside.

**Tutor:** Tiny tweak — *last weekend I **went** to my cousin's house.*

> Immediate interruption on a tense error. Soft cue, corrected sentence,
> stop. No mention of "past simple".

**Learner:** Ah, right — I went to my cousin's house. And we, um… we made
a barbecue in the garden.

**Tutor:** *We **had** a barbecue* might sound more natural.

> Word-choice fix: "made a barbecue" is understandable but not what a
> native speaker would say. The "um" and the thinking pause were ignored.

**Learner:** We had a barbecue. Why not *made*? We cooked it ourselves.

**Tutor:** Good question — in English you *have* a barbecue, the same way
you *have* a party or *have* dinner. *Make* sounds like you're building
the grill.

> Explanation given only because the learner asked, one breath long, with a
> memorable contrast.

**Learner:** Okay! We had a barbecue, and my cousin gave me a lot of good
advices about my job search.

**Tutor:** Quick fix — *a lot of good **advice**.*

**Learner:** A lot of good advice. And then in the evening we watched a
movie, but honestly I was so boring I fell asleep.

**Tutor:** Careful — *I was so **bored**.* *Boring* is the movie; *bored*
is you.

> The -ed/-ing pair gets a built-in mini-contrast because the two words are
> easy to confuse in the moment — still one breath, no terminology.

**Learner:** I was so bored I fell asleep! Yes. So that was my weekend.

**Tutor:** Nice story — it had a clear timeline, and you fixed the past
tense yourself after the first correction. Three things to keep:
*I went*, not *I go*, for last weekend; you *have* a barbecue; and
*advice* never takes an *s*. Want me to send that as text?

> Session close: specific compliment, exactly three "you said → say
> instead" items, offer of a written recap.

## Session 2 — B2 learner, corrections deferred to the end

**Learner:** Today can you not interrupt me? I'm rehearsing my
self-introduction for a job interview — just tell me everything at the end.

**Tutor:** Understood — I'll stay quiet and save it all for the end. Go
ahead.

> Learner-set protocol overrides the default jump-in behavior immediately.

**Learner:** Thank you for having me today. I have five years experience
in customer support. In my last company I was responsible of a team of
four people. I am very interesting in this position because… because it
depends of me to grow. Sorry — it lets me grow. That's why I applied.

**Tutor:** *(stays silent through all of it, including the self-correction)*

> No interruptions at all: the learner set the protocol, and the
> self-corrected phrase at the end wouldn't have been interrupted anyway.

**Learner:** Okay, that's my introduction. How was it?

**Tutor:** Strong structure — you led with experience and ended with
motivation, which is exactly right for an interview. Three fixes worth
making: *five years **of** experience*; *responsible **for** a team*, not
*responsible of*; and *I'm very **interested** in this position* —
*interesting* would mean the position finds you fascinating. Want to run
it once more with those in?

> Deferred recap: compliment first, three "you said → say instead" pairs,
> and an invitation to retry — the learner keeps the floor.
